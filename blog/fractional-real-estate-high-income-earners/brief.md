## Focus keyword
fractional real estate for high income earners

## ICP
High-earning professional whose problem is after-tax return, not deal access. Likely accredited or close to it; values simplicity and hates surprise paperwork.

## Content Angle
Tax-first walkthrough: pass-through depreciation and what it does to K-1 taxable income vs cash received (passive activity limits stated), the possible Section 199A QBI angle, accredited thresholds and what Reg D 506(c) opens vs what Reg A already offers without accreditation, multi-state K-1 filings, and the honest paperwork cost. Close with when a plain REIT ETF beats all of it for a high earner who values simplicity.

## Human Anchors

### A. Scenario
Linda, a 45-year-old surgeon in Chicago, whose problem is not finding investments but keeping what they earn after tax. Scenario framing only; no testimonial claims.

### B. POV Anchor
We think high earners are the most underserved readers in this category: every platform wants their money, and almost nobody writes for the part of the decision that actually moves for them, which is the tax line.

### C. Contrarian Note
The standard pitch to high earners is access: exclusive deals behind the accredited wall. Access is the weakest reason to invest. The structural reason a pass-through property position can suit a high earner is boring and printed on a K-1: depreciation timing, not deal exclusivity.
