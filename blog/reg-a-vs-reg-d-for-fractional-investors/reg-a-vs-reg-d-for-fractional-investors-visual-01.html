Reg A vs Reg D

The framework decides who is allowed to invest.

Every US fractional offering sits under one of two SEC exemptions. The choice is structural: it sets who can buy in, how much disclosure you get, and what the issuer must keep reporting afterwards.

Regulation A, Tier 2

Open to the public, capped at $75 million per 12 months.
Non-accredited investors can participate, limited to 10 percent of the greater of annual income or net worth per offering. The issuer files a Form 1-K annual report, public on EDGAR. Qualification takes months.

Regulation D, Rule 506(c)

Accredited investors only, no offering-size limit.
General solicitation is permitted, but every investor must be verified as accredited. Faster to bring to market, with less public disclosure attached.

Neither one is safer. Reg A trades months of SEC qualification for retail access. Reg D trades public access for speed. Check which framework an offering uses on EDGAR before you invest, because it tells you what you are entitled to see.
