## Focus keyword
reinvesting fractional real estate distributions

## ICP
Existing fractional investor whose distributions land in checking and evaporate. Wants the concrete compounding difference and the practical mechanics of reinvesting.

## Content Angle
Precomputed math only: $10,000 at 7% reinvested annually reaches about $19,672 in ten years vs $17,000 taken as cash, a gap of about $2,672; the $5,000 case is $9,836 vs $8,500. Then the frictions the calculator never shows: minimum ticket sizes stranding cash, K-1 multiplication with every new position, distribution pauses, yield variability. Practical patterns: batching distributions until they clear a minimum, same-platform vs spreading.

## Human Anchors

### A. Scenario
Karen, a 39-year-old dental hygienist in Nashville, whose distributions have been landing in a checking account and quietly becoming groceries. Scenario framing only; no testimonial claims.

### B. POV Anchor
We think auto-reinvestment is the single most underrated feature request in this category, and the honest reason platforms are slow to build it is that reinvestment fragments positions into odd lots that are harder to administer.

### C. Contrarian Note
Compounding gets described as magic. It is arithmetic, and in fractional real estate the arithmetic fights three frictions the compound-interest calculator never shows: minimum ticket sizes that strand cash, K-1s that multiply with every new position, and distributions that pause when a property does.
