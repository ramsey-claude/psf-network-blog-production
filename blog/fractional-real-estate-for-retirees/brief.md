## Focus keyword
fractional real estate for retirees

## ICP
Retiree or near-retiree evaluating fractional income against a fixed monthly budget. The binding constraints are liquidity and tax mechanics, not yield.

## Content Angle
Three honest lenses: income (what 4 to 9% platform-reported yields mean against a real budget), liquidity (the 5 to 10 year lockup against late-life liquidity needs; secondary markets are thin), and tax mechanics (K-1 timing including the September extension, possible multi-state filings, depreciation effects). Position REITs as the more liquid alternative for income-focused retirees who need daily liquidity.

## Human Anchors

### A. Scenario
Paul, 66, a retired teacher in Phoenix, comparing his monthly budget against what a fractional position would actually pay. Scenario framing only; no testimonial claims.

### B. POV Anchor
We would rather lose a retiree's investment than their liquidity; if the money might be needed for health or family inside five years, we think it does not belong on our platform or any platform like ours.

### C. Contrarian Note
The industry pitches income products to retirees on the yield number. For a retiree the binding constraint is almost never yield; it is the calendar: a 5 to 10 year lockup against a time horizon where plans change fast, and a K-1 that can arrive in September against a filing rhythm built on 1099s.
