## Focus keyword
fractional real estate as a bond substitute

## ICP
Income-focused investor wondering whether property distributions can replace part of a bond allocation. Needs the structural contrast, not a yield-vs-yield table.

## Content Angle
Structural contrast: contractual coupon vs variable distribution, defined maturity vs 5 to 10 year open-ended hold, Treasury government backing vs no insurance of any kind (FDIC covers bank deposits, not securities), daily bond-market liquidity vs thin secondaries. Where the substitute case genuinely holds: long-horizon income money that can tolerate variability, diversification away from rate-driven bond pricing. Where it fails: ladders, known future liabilities, emergency reserves.

## Human Anchors

### A. Scenario
Beth, a 58-year-old controller in San Diego, staring at the income line of her portfolio and wondering whether property could do a bond job. Scenario framing only; no testimonial claims.

### B. POV Anchor
We sell the property side of this comparison and we will still say it plainly: money that must arrive on a schedule belongs in instruments with a maturity date, and no rental distribution has one.

### C. Contrarian Note
The comparison everyone runs is yield against yield, and it is the wrong axis. A bond is a contract: a stated coupon and a date your principal returns. A fractional distribution is a residual: what is left after a building expenses, arriving only while things go well. Comparing the two on yield alone is comparing a promise to a hope with better upside.
